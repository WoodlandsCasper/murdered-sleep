CASE FILE 01

The Case Opens

Read the Clue
Breaking News, Sleep Detectives! Something terrible has happened at Casper. At exactly 3:13 a.m. on October 26, the overnight security system recorded a strange sequence of events. When the morning team arrived, the showroom looked like someone had been very busy while everyone else was sleeping. Investigators discovered: • Three mattresses had been adjusted into different positions. • One Adjustable Base remote was missing. • A Casper pillow had been moved from its display. • A half-finished cup of coffee sat beside a demo bed. • The showroom lights had been turned back on. • And written on the back-room whiteboard: “SLEEP IS OVERRATED.” Nothing was damaged. Nothing appeared to be stolen. So the Sleep Police have one big question: Was someone trying to destroy sleep... or demonstrate something? Six suspects had connections to the showroom that night. Over the next five days, you’ll receive new evidence. Study the clues. Solve the daily detective games. Watch for false leads. And remember: Not every clue is telling you the truth. Your job is to figure out: WHO MURDERED SLEEP? The case is officially open.

THE CRIME

What Happened to Sleep?

Classified Briefing
At exactly 3:13 a.m. on October 26, the Spirit of Perfect Sleep was murdered — or so the Sleep Police believe. When the morning team arrived at the Casper showroom, Sleep was gone. The scene was strange but oddly specific: • Three mattresses had been adjusted into different positions. • Every Adjustable Base in the showroom had been raised. • One Adjustable Base remote was missing. • One Casper pillow had been moved from its display. • A half-finished cup of coffee sat beside a demo bed. • The showroom lights had been turned back on. • A footprint was found covered in pillow feathers. • The clock had stopped at exactly 3:13 a.m. • On the back-room whiteboard, someone had written: “SLEEP IS OVERRATED.” Nothing valuable appeared to be stolen. The Sleep Police believe someone deliberately interfered with the showroom's sleep environment — and then tried to make the scene look like something else. Six people had access to the showroom that night. Your mission is not simply to find someone who looks suspicious. Your mission is to determine who had the opportunity, motive, and connection to the evidence — and ultimately answer: WHO MURDERED SLEEP?
SUSPECT FILES

The Six Suspects

Every suspect has a reason to be investigated. Some may be innocent. Some may be hiding something. And one may be responsible.

Read Carefully

Olivia “Overtime”

Role: The hard-working closer.

Known for: Working late and believing the work should come before sleep.

Statement: “I’ll sleep when the work is done.”

Last known activity: Left the showroom at 11:47 p.m.

Possible motive: She may genuinely believe sleep gets in the way of productivity.

Carl “Caffeine”

Role: The coffee-and-productivity enthusiast.

Known for: Running on coffee and insisting that productivity never sleeps.

Statement: “Productivity doesn’t sleep.”

Evidence connection: Coffee was found beside a demo bed.

Possible motive: He may want people awake, alert, and productive around the clock.

Polly Pillow

Role: The pillow expert.

Known for: Constantly adjusting, refluffing, and correcting pillow displays.

Statement: “Nobody respects the pillow.”

Evidence connection: A pillow had been moved, and pillow feathers were found at the scene.

Possible motive: She may have wanted to prove that the pillow is the most important part of the sleep experience.

Barry Base

Role: The Adjustable Base expert.

Known for: Demonstrating Zero Gravity and believing the future of sleep is adjustable.

Statement: “Flat beds are yesterday’s news.”

Evidence connection: Every Adjustable Base had been raised, and a remote was missing.

Last known activity: Left the showroom at 9:45 p.m.

Possible motive: He wants everyone to abandon ordinary flat beds.

Nora Nightshift

Role: The after-hours regular.

Known for: Knowing the showroom routines and being comfortable after closing.

Statement: “I forgot my keys.”

Last known activity: Left at 11:02 p.m., but later returned after midnight.

Possible motive: She knew the showroom, its routines, and how to move around after hours.

Dr. Nightmare

Role: The sleep scientist.

Known for: Studying sleep, dreams, and the effects of sleep deprivation.

Statement: “There is still so much we don't understand about sleep.”

Evidence connection: Her research could benefit from an unusual disruption of normal sleep patterns.

Possible motive: Endless research — even if that means pushing the limits of what people can endure without sleep.

Detective reminder: A motive is not proof. An alibi is not automatically innocence. And a suspicious clue may have been planted to point you in the wrong direction. Keep track of what you know versus what you merely suspect.
